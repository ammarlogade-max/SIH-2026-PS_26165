Loss of Well Control
Recently, during a workover operation, a well that had been shut-in for approximately10
years began to flow as a sand bridge was removed by a nitrogen washing.Gas flowed
between the tubing string and a 7-inch casing and over the casing stub over which the
tubing head was installed.Because there was no seal assembly in the wellhead, the gas
pressure was exerted on a structurally compromised wellhead injection port because of
corrosion.The port fitting failed and gas flowed through the port to the atmosphere,
resulting in a minor condensate spill into the Gulf.The well was shortly thereafter killed.
An MMS investigation of the incident concluded that the causes of the incident, in addition
to the immediate structurally deficient equipment, were, in part, (1) failure to inspect and
maintain equipment, (2) failure to conduct a Job Safety Analysis (JSA) for workover
activities, (3) failure to provide clear and specific instructions to contracted personnel, and
(4) failure to communicate with the contracted operator of the lease.
It is therefore recommended that:
Lessees and Operators should review their well maintenance policies and activities.1.
Lessees and Operators should communicate clearly and in writing what is expected of
all of their field representatives.
2.
JSA’s should be performed for all tasks involving hazards.3.
